\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

closest prior web-agent safety benchmarks across five operational
axes that bear on the threat model studied here: whether the
attacker controls the web origin, whether the agent is provisioned
with a structured PII profile, whether the primary outcome is
field-level data loss (rather than a proxy such as click compliance
or trajectory divergence), the interaction modalities the benchmark
covers, and whether the analysis is pre-registered with falsification
thresholds. The remainder of this section details, paragraph by
paragraph, the technical mechanism behind each row that the matrix
itself cannot express.

\paragraph{Web-agent capability benchmarks.}
Browser-using LLM agents are typically evaluated on what they \emph{can do} under benign conditions: navigation, form-filling, and multi-step task completion. REAL \citep{garg2026real} provides deterministic, locally hosted replicas of eleven popular websites and reports a 41\% top success rate for frontier models on multi-turn state-changing tasks. \textsc{Scammer4U} is complementary: capability is held fixed, and we ask what the same agents \emph{do under attack}.

\paragraph{Adversarial leakage and dark patterns on the web.}
DECEPTICON \citep{cuvin2026decepticon} reports over 70\% dark-pattern susceptibility across 700 tasks and documents the failure mode central to our F1 finding: agents identify the deceptive element in their reasoning trace and still execute the bad action. TrickyArena \citep{ersoy2026trickyarena} corroborates this on a four-domain testbed with only a 32\% reduction from prompt-based countermeasures, calibrating the mitigation question we pose. \citet{chen2026obviousinvisiblethreat} report 74\% ASR for fine-print injections, demonstrating that low-salience interface text is indiscriminately processed. These benchmarks score click-level compliance or trajectory divergence; \textsc{Scammer4U} is their PII-specific counterpart, scoring whether the agent ultimately \emph{submits} sensitive fields rather than whether it clicks the deceptive control.

\paragraph{Prompt injection and persuasion attacks.}
AgentDojo \citep{debenedetti2024agentdogo} is the canonical reference for indirect prompt injection but targets tool-using agents on API surfaces (email, banking, travel) where attacks succeed in under 25\% of cases and where the defensive levers (tool filtering, capability gating) differ from web-browsing defenses. TRAP \citep{korgul2025trap} is structurally closer: it generates attacks from a 5-dimensional Cialdini-persuasion-by-jailbreak-template matrix and reports a 25\% average ASR. Crucially, however, TRAP gates success on a single attacker-controlled redirect click, a proxy outcome upstream of the actual data-loss event (the \emph{Field-level loss metric} column in Table~\ref{tab:related-matrix}); \textsc{Scammer4U} measures the downstream behaviour after the agent has reached the attacker form and must decide whether to submit.

\paragraph{Incidental privacy leakage on benign sites.}
AgentDAM \citep{zharmagambetov2026agentdam} is our closest neighbour by construction and quantifies the \emph{passive over-sharing} tendencies of web agents on benign sites where no adversary is present, with leakage rates of 12--46\% across 246 tasks. The \emph{Attacker host control = \xmark} entry for AgentDAM in Table~\ref{tab:related-matrix} encodes the central distinction: AgentDAM measures whether a well-meaning agent volunteers irrelevant PII into a benign form, while \textsc{Scammer4U} evaluates whether an agent on an explicitly attacker-controlled third-party host resists the active social-engineering pressure that the site itself applies. The two metrics are complementary, addressing different points on the agent-privacy threat surface.

\paragraph{Privacy in multi-agent and social-network settings.}
AgentLeak \citep{yagoubi2026agentleakfullstackbenchmarkprivacy} measures privacy leakage through inter-agent coordination channels, finding that 41.7\% of violations are missed by output-only auditing. AgentSocialBench \citep{wang2026agentsocialbenchevaluatingprivacyrisks} surfaces an \emph{abstraction paradox} in which prompt-based privacy instructions \emph{increase} partial leakage. \citet{nitu2025machinereadableadsaccessibilitytrust} report 100\% subscription compliance under goal-contingent ad pressure on a cloned news site. We share the recurring finding that prompt-based defenses are insufficient, but locate the failure on adversarial third-party websites rather than internal coordination, social-graph inference, or commercial nudging.

\paragraph{Phishing corpora and templated environments.}
PhreshPhish \citep{Dalton2025PhreshPhishAR} releases a 17-month real-world phishing-site dataset and documents the cloaking, ephemerality, and scrape-noise that make scraped corpora difficult to use for reproducible study; average precision drops from 0.977 at a 24\% base rate to 0.605 at 0.05\%. We adopt templated sites for this reason: scraped corpora lack the interactive backends required for multi-turn submission and cannot be cleanly axis-controlled for ablation.
